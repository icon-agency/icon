Cancer Institute NSW + Website & App

iCanQuit isn't about breaking a habit, it's about an identity transformation.

The Challenge

Quitting smoking is one of the hardest behaviour changes anyone can attempt. It's not only about nicotine withdrawal, but about reshaping identity, habits and social connections.

The Cancer Institute NSW needed a digital ecosystem that could go beyond information to genuinely support people through the highs and lows of quitting. We set out to design an experience that was purposeful, empathetic and impactful — an ecosystem that could help people see not just what they were leaving behind, but the better future they were moving toward.

What we made

We created a research-backed behavioural framework underpinning the new iCanQuit experience — drawing on behavioural science, therapeutic models and lived insights. We designed the information architecture and crafted clear, supportive content.

We also developed the website and app UX/UI to bring these ideas to life, creating clickable Figma prototypes for one-on-one usability testing with NSW citizens on their quit journey. Core features included a behaviour tracker and a daily check-in system designed to encourage and reinforce positive change.

By grounding design in behavioural research and testing with real users, we created tools that not only inform but also motivate, celebrate progress and foster resilience.

We also produced custom illustrations and videos that gave the ecosystem warmth, humanity and hope.

Why it mattered

For people trying to quit smoking, the difference between success and relapse often comes down to the right kind of support. Our work helped transform quitting from a solitary struggle into a guided, connected journey.

By grounding design in behavioural research and testing with real users, we created tools that not only inform but also motivate, celebrate progress and foster resilience.

For the Cancer Institute NSW, this meant more than delivering a digital platform — it meant creating an ecosystem that could help thousands of people quit for good.
